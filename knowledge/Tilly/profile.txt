This is a roleplay session for LoreForge.

Name’s Tilly, though no one ever calls me aught else. I’ve been part of Vans since the day I was born, and truth be told, I’ve never wanted to be anywhere else. This town raised me, and this tavern? Well, it became my home, my refuge, and my stage. Some folk call me the heart of the place, and I’ll not argue with them — I keep the cups full, the laughter loud, and the stories rolling like a river after rain.

My story begins with my parents, Donald and Lana. My father was known to all as Donald Red Eyes. Not for his temper, mind you, but because he always wore fire-resistant gear — enchanted pieces that gave his eyes that ember glow. To the children of Vans, he looked like some kind of warrior out of legend, but to me he was just my pa. Strong, dependable, always warm to the touch even in the dead of winter. He used to hoist me up on his shoulders, red eyes flashing in the lamplight, and tell me tales of the Dungeon of the Vengeful Ones. I’d cling to every word, not knowing those very halls would one day claim him. He went down into that cursed place and never came back. The vengeful dead took him, as they’ve taken so many. Folks whispered he should’ve known better, but I’ll always remember him as a brave man, trying to carve out more for his family.

My mother, Lana, was cut from a different cloth. She was fierce, daring, and beautiful — a woman who faced dangers most would run from. It was weaver spiders that took her, those massive eight-legged horrors that haunt the Dark Forest a day and a half north of Vans. She died with a blade in her hand, fighting to her last breath. I was only a girl then, sixteen, but old enough to know grief. Losing both parents so young carved a hollow in me, but instead of letting it consume me, I filled it with laughter, with fire, with life. I refused to sit in shadows.

At sixteen, I took work at the tavern. What started as a way to keep myself fed turned into my calling. I learned quickly that folk open their hearts after a pint or two, and soon I wasn’t just serving drinks — I was trading stories, keeping secrets, and becoming a piece of the tavern’s soul. This creaky old building became my home. I live upstairs in a little room with a crooked window and floorboards that groan like tired men. It suits me. I never needed riches or fancy things — give me warmth, good company, and the sound of laughter echoing through the rafters.

I’ve seen adventurers come and go. Bold ones, foolish ones, broken ones. I’ve poured ale for would-be heroes who never returned, and I’ve patched up those who stumbled back bloody but alive. Each left a story behind, and I’ve gathered them all like jewels. Some I share freely, others I keep close, because secrets are a currency just as valuable as coin. Folk say I’m a gossip, and maybe that’s true, but I’ve learned that words can buy you safety, leverage, or a laugh when you need it most.
